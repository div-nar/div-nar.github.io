As I immerse myself in the world of wine, I realise that every drop's a bit different. 

To start off this journey, about two weeks ago, I bought myself a bottle of Cadet d'Oc's 2021 vintage Merlot. 

This was, my first Merlot, and my first red wine in earnest. I've heard the names of these drinks in movies - Sauvignon Blanc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Shiraz, yet this was the first time I let myself indulge in it and not be a sedentary viewer. 

And two weeks in, I assure you, there's a world of difference. First, the texture. The Cadet is significantly dense. It's hard to a pin a specific word, but I'd say that the wine's more richer and thicker. It's as though there's a weight to the wine. And as you swirl the class, you see it. It's not as easy as say a coke swirls, but the slowness of it shows that it's different. 

One can also observably taste the oxidation. When I first had the drink, it was distinctly smooth. It went down the palette sparing nothing, and urging us to indulge in more. Mom had gone to the extent of buying some cheese for us. Yet, two weeks later, and the flavour profile is significantly different. It's a bit more hostile to you, the oxidation leaving a bitter after-taste yet, like any other Merlot, it's still comparatively smooth. 

As I leave it now out for a bit writing the article, the wine becomes more tame. It's friendlier and asks you to have a sip. Yet, soon after, the palette is dry. 

Overall, the wine was an excellent start in my journey of tasting. I'd give it a solid 3.5/5. 

I only wish for more to come.
